\section{Conclusion and Outlook}
\label{sec:conclusion}

% Summary of Work
This work presented a comprehensive investigation of multimodal AI approaches for clinical decision support in tropical and infectious disease diagnosis. We developed and evaluated three distinct methodologies: Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) for dynamic knowledge integration, parameter-efficient fine-tuning for domain adaptation, and hybrid architectures combining both approaches.

% Key Findings (Customize based on your actual results)
\subsection{Key Findings}

Our evaluation revealed several important insights:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Complementary Strengths:} RAG and fine-tuning approaches demonstrate complementary strengths, with RAG excelling on rare diseases through external knowledge retrieval, while fine-tuned models capture learned diagnostic patterns for common conditions more effectively.
    
    \item \textbf{Hybrid Advantage:} The hybrid approach combining RAG with fine-tuning achieved superior performance compared to either method alone, validating our hypothesis that integration of dynamic knowledge retrieval with learned representations provides comprehensive diagnostic coverage.
    
    \item \textbf{Multimodal Integration:} Incorporation of multiple data modalities (text, images, structured data) significantly improved diagnostic accuracy compared to single-modality approaches, demonstrating the importance of comprehensive clinical information synthesis.
    
    \item \textbf{Interpretability Benefits:} RAG-based systems provided enhanced interpretability through retrieved evidence and explicit knowledge grounding, facilitating clinical trust and adoption.
\end{itemize}

% Limitations
\subsection{Limitations}

Despite promising results, our work has several limitations that should be acknowledged:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Dataset Constraints:} Limited dataset size and potential geographic/demographic biases may affect generalizability across diverse patient populations.
    
    \item \textbf{Evaluation Scope:} Evaluation was conducted retrospectively on curated datasets; prospective clinical validation is needed to assess real-world performance.
    
    \item \textbf{Computational Resources:} Resource constraints limited extensive hyperparameter exploration and evaluation of larger model architectures.
    
    \item \textbf{Expert Validation:} Limited involvement of clinical domain experts in comprehensive error analysis and system validation.
\end{itemize}

% Future Directions
\subsection{Future Directions}

Several promising directions emerge from this work:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Enhanced Multimodal Fusion:} Investigate advanced fusion architectures and attention mechanisms for better integration of heterogeneous data types.
    
    \item \textbf{Active Learning:} Develop active learning strategies to efficiently identify and acquire training examples for underrepresented disease categories.
    
    \item \textbf{Explainability Enhancements:} Improve interpretability through visualization of attention patterns, decision pathways, and uncertainty quantification.
    
    \item \textbf{Clinical Deployment:} Conduct prospective clinical trials to evaluate real-world performance, workflow integration, and impact on patient outcomes.
    
    \item \textbf{Expanded Disease Coverage:} Extend the system to broader disease categories and integrate with existing clinical decision support infrastructure.
    
    \item \textbf{Continual Learning:} Implement mechanisms for continuous model updating as new medical knowledge and clinical data become available.
\end{itemize}

% Closing Statement
\subsection{Concluding Remarks}

This research demonstrates the potential of multimodal AI systems to enhance clinical decision support for tropical and infectious disease diagnosis. By combining the complementary strengths of retrieval-augmented generation and fine-tuning approaches, we can develop systems that leverage both extensive medical knowledge repositories and learned diagnostic patterns. While significant challenges remain before clinical deployment, our findings provide valuable insights into effective architectures and evaluation methodologies for medical AI systems. As these technologies mature, they hold promise for improving diagnostic accuracy, reducing diagnostic delays, and ultimately enhancing patient care, particularly in resource-limited settings where access to specialized expertise is constrained.
