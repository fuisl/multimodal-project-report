\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

% Background and Context
% The timely and accurate diagnosis of tropical and infectious diseases remains a critical challenge in global health, particularly in resource-limited settings where access to specialized medical expertise may be limited. Traditional diagnostic approaches often rely on clinical examination, laboratory tests, and medical imaging, each providing valuable but incomplete information when considered in isolation. The complexity of disease presentations, overlapping symptomatology across different conditions, and the need for rapid decision-making place significant demands on healthcare providers.

Tropical infectious diseases remain a major public health burden in numerous regions worldwide, particularly in regions with limited healthcare resources and specialist expertise. 
Early and accurate diagnosis is critical to reduce transmission, and improve patient outcomes. However, in resource-constrained settings, symptoms are often nonspecific and overlapping among various tropical diseases, complicating the diagnostic process.
Diagnostic decisions rely on heterogeneous data sources—including clinical history, laboratory tests, and medical imaging—which often provide incomplete evidence when considered separately, increasing uncertainty and cognitive burden for healthcare providers. 


% Advances in AI and Multimodal Learning
Recent advances in Artificial Intelligence, especially Large Language Models(LLMs) and Large Multimodal Models (LMMs), have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in understanding and reasoning over heterogeneous data types, including text, images, and structured medical data. When adapted to healthcare, these models offer the potential to integrate diverse clinical inputs and provide context-aware diagnostic support.
However, for tropical and infectious diseases, current GenAI systems remain limited in multimodal integration and contextual understanding, leading to challenges in delivering accurate, reliable, and contextually relevant reasoning for complex medical diagnosis. Furthermore, the scarcity of high-quality multimodal datasets, inhibits the effective training and evaluating these models in real-world clinical scenarios.
Interpretability and trustworthiness are also critical concerns, as AI-generated recommendations must be understood and validated by clinicians to ensure patient safety and ethical standards.

For these reasons, there is a pressing need to develop robust multimodal AI systems integrating various data sources and types to enhance the diagnostic accuracy and reasoning capabilities. Multiple approaches, including Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), fine-tuning of pre-trained models, and hybrid strategies, should be implemented and rigorously evaluated to identify optimal solutions for clinical decision support in tropical infectious diseases.
Comprehensive evaluation and analysis of model performance is also essential to tackle challenges related to generalization and interpretability.

% Motivation for this Work

% Research Contributions
The key contributions of this research include:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Implementation and comparative evaluation of three approaches for clinical decision support in tropical infectious diseases: Retrieval-Augmented Generation (LightRAG), domain-specific fine-tuning using NVIDIA NeMo, and a hybrid approach architectures that integrate both strategies.
    \item Construction of a standardized benchmark multimodal dataset combining clinical text, medical images, and structured patient data for tropical and infectious disease diagnosis.
    \item Development of a unified fine-tuning dataset with structured diagnostic answers and step-by-step reasoning, formatted in a USMLE-inspired style and aligned with the MedCaseReasoning workflow.
    \item Design of a rigorous evaluation framework assessing model performance using RAGAS.
    \item Implementation of an interactive interface with step-level reasoning monitoring using LangGraph.
\end{itemize}
