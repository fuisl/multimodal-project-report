\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

% Background and Context
The timely and accurate diagnosis of tropical and infectious diseases remains a critical challenge in global health, particularly in resource-limited settings where access to specialized medical expertise may be limited. Traditional diagnostic approaches often rely on clinical examination, laboratory tests, and medical imaging, each providing valuable but incomplete information when considered in isolation. The complexity of disease presentations, overlapping symptomatology across different conditions, and the need for rapid decision-making place significant demands on healthcare providers.

% Advances in AI and Multimodal Learning
Recent advances in artificial intelligence, particularly large language models (LLMs) and large multimodal models (LMMs), have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in understanding and reasoning over diverse data types including text, images, and structured medical data. These models, when properly adapted to medical domains, offer the potential to integrate multiple sources of clinical information, access vast repositories of medical knowledge, and provide evidence-based diagnostic support to clinicians.

% Motivation for this Work
This work explores the application of state-of-the-art multimodal AI systems to clinical decision support for tropical and infectious disease diagnosis. We investigate three distinct but complementary approaches: Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) for dynamic knowledge integration, model fine-tuning for domain-specific adaptation, and hybrid architectures that combine the strengths of both methods. Our focus on multimodal integration addresses the reality of clinical practice, where diagnosis typically requires synthesizing information from patient history, physical examination findings, laboratory results, and medical imaging.

% Research Contributions
The key contributions of this research include:
\begin{itemize}
    \item A comprehensive multimodal dataset combining clinical text, medical images, and structured patient data for tropical and infectious disease diagnosis
    \item Implementation and comparative evaluation of RAG, fine-tuning, and hybrid approaches for multimodal clinical decision support
    \item An extensive evaluation framework incorporating multiple performance metrics, stratified analysis, and error pattern investigation
    \item Insights into the relative strengths and limitations of different AI approaches for medical diagnosis, with particular emphasis on rare disease detection and diagnostic reasoning quality
\end{itemize}
